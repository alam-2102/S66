Topic IV — Laws, Regulations and Guidelines of the exam

The largest block and the one with least overlap with the Series 7. If anything is going to decide this exam, it is this tab.

Who has to register — the four persons

PersonRegisters withCore test
Investment Adviser (IA)State, or SEC if federal covered ABC: Advice about securities, as a Business, for Compensation — all three
Investment Adviser Rep (IAR)State (always — even reps of federal covered IAs) Natural person associated with an IA who advises, solicits, or supervises those who do
Broker-Dealer (BD)State and SEC Effects securities transactions for the account of others or its own
Agent of a BDState Natural person representing a BD or issuer in effecting securities transactions

The split that gets tested: the IA registers where it has a place of business or enough clients; the IAR always registers with the state, including when their firm is federal covered. A federal covered IA notice files with the state and pays the fee — it does not register there.

Federal covered vs. state-registered IA — the dollar thresholds

Assets under managementRegisters with
Under $100 millionState
$100m – $110mOptional — may choose either (the buffer)
$110 million or moreSEC — must register federally
Drops below $90 millionMust withdraw from SEC and register with the state

Also federal covered regardless of AUM: advisers to registered investment companies, and advisers not required to register in any state. An adviser with under $25m in a state that has no registration provision is federal covered by default.

Exclusions — not an IA at all

The classic mnemonic is LATE — these professions are excluded when advice is incidental to their practice and no separate fee is charged for it:

  • Lawyers · Accountants · Teachers · Engineers
  • Banks, savings institutions and trust companies (banks are excluded as IAs; note the contrast with BDs, where banks are also excluded)
  • Broker-dealers and their agents, when advice is incidental and there is no special compensation for it
  • Publishers of bona fide, regular, general-circulation publications — the test is that the advice is impersonal and not tailored to a client's situation
  • Federal covered advisers (excluded from state registration, notice filing aside)

The trap: charge a separate, identifiable fee for the advice and the exclusion evaporates. An accountant who bills separately for a financial plan is an IA.

The brokerage / advisory line

A BD that charges commissions for executing trades and gives incidental advice is a BD. The moment it charges a fee based on assets, wraps advice into a separate charge, or holds itself out as an adviser, it is acting as an IA and owes the fiduciary standard rather than Reg BI's best-interest standard. Fee-based wrap accounts are advisory business.

Agent registration — the exclusions that get tested

  • A person representing an issuer in an exempt transaction or with an exempt security is not an agent
  • Clerical and ministerial employees are not agents — until they take an order
  • A person representing an issuer who receives no commission and sells only to existing employees, partners or directors is not an agent
  • Administrative personnel of a BD are still agents if they take orders. This one catches people.

Lack of experience alone is not grounds for denial. The Administrator may not deny a registration solely because the applicant lacks experience — it can be considered alongside training and knowledge, but it cannot stand on its own.

Custody — what counts and what it triggers

Custody means holding client funds or securities, or having any authority to obtain possession of them. It includes a general power of attorney, acting as trustee, and direct fee deduction from a client account.

  • Receiving a cheque made out to a third party and forwarding it within 3 business days is not custody
  • Receiving a cheque made out to the adviser is custody
  • Inadvertently receiving client funds and returning them within 3 business days is not custody
  • Custody requires: notice to the Administrator, a qualified custodian, quarterly account statements, and the annual surprise audit
  • Fee deduction is custody, but is relieved of the surprise audit if the custodian sends statements directly to the client and the adviser sends an invoice showing the calculation

Discretion — and the three things that are not

Discretion is authority over Asset, Amount, or Action — what to buy, how much, whether to buy or sell. Time and price alone are not discretion — “buy 100 XYZ when you think it's right today” is an unsolicited order good for that day only, not discretionary authority.

  • IA: may exercise discretion orally for the first 10 business days, then needs written authority
  • BD: needs prior written authorization before the first discretionary trade

That 10-day asymmetry is a favourite exam question.

Contracts — what an advisory contract must say

  • In writing (state-registered IAs)
  • Services, term, fee and the formula for computing it
  • That the contract may not be assigned without client consent
  • For a partnership, that the client is notified of any change in the minority membership within a reasonable time
  • No prepayment refund terms omitted — state how prepaid fees are refunded
  • No performance-based fees, except for a qualified client: $1.1m under management with the adviser, or $2.2m net worth (indexed — confirm the current figures against your Kaplan text)

Brochure rule

Form ADV Part 2 (or an equivalent brochure) must be delivered not later than entry into the contract. Under state rules the older 48-hour test still appears: deliver 48 hours before entering the contract, or at entry provided the client has 5 business days to cancel without penalty. Annual delivery of a summary of material changes within 120 days of fiscal year end.

Impersonal advice under $500/year and advice to registered investment companies are excepted from brochure delivery.

Prohibited and unethical practices — Topic IV·H·4

The densest single block on the exam and the highest question yield per hour of study. Treat it the way the Series 7 treated options.
  • Loans to or from clients — prohibited, unless the client is a bank, an affiliate, or a broker-dealer in the business of lending
  • Sharing in profits and losses — an IAR may never share; a BD agent may only with written approval of the firm, a joint account, and sharing proportionate to capital contributed
  • Guaranteeing against loss — always prohibited, no exceptions
  • Churning / excessive trading — judged against the client's objectives and resources, not a fixed turnover number
  • Selling away — private securities transactions without firm knowledge and approval
  • Market manipulation — matched orders, wash trades, painting the tape
  • Insider trading — the duty runs to material non-public information; tippee liability follows the tipper's breach and personal benefit
  • Client confidentiality — no disclosure without consent, except to the Administrator, by subpoena, or as required by law
  • Personal securities transactions — front-running prohibited; initial holdings and quarterly reports required under the code of ethics
  • Outside securities accounts — disclosure and firm consent
  • Political contributions — the pay-to-play rule: a 2-year time-out on compensated advisory services to a government client after a triggering contribution. De minimis exceptions: $350 per election to a candidate you can vote for, $150 if you cannot
  • Exploitation of vulnerable adults — the NASAA model act permits a delay of disbursement on reasonable belief of exploitation, with notification to the Administrator and Adult Protective Services
  • Unlawful representation concerning registration — registration never implies the Administrator approved the merits. Saying or implying it is prohibited.
  • Performance guarantees — prohibited; so is cherry-picked or non-representative performance advertising

Definitions — security, and what is not one

The Howey test: an investment of money, in a common enterprise, with an expectation of profits, derived primarily from the efforts of others.

Not securities: insurance policies with fixed benefits, fixed annuities, endowment policies, collectibles, precious metals, commodities and futures contracts, currency, condominiums bought as a residence, retirement plans themselves (an IRA is an account, not a security).

Securities that surprise people: variable annuities, variable life, options on securities, warrants, rights, limited partnership interests, interests in oil and gas programs, and investment contracts of all kinds.

Exempt securities vs. exempt transactions

An exempt security is exempt because of what it is. An exempt transaction is exempt because of how it is sold. The antifraud provisions apply to both — exemption never exempts you from fraud liability.

The unsolicited order exemption is the most-tested single line in this block. The Administrator may require the client to sign an acknowledgement that the order was unsolicited.

Registration methods for securities

  • Notification / filing — for established issuers with a track record
  • Coordination — filed with SEC and state simultaneously; effective when the federal registration is, provided the state filing has been on file 10 days and the price terms have been on file 2 full business days
  • Qualification — the fallback, state only; effective when the Administrator says so

Topic I — the formulas of the exam

Only questions, but the most formula-dense part of the exam and the cheapest block to lose points in. These have to be cold.

Time value of money

FV = PV × (1 + r)n PV = FV ÷ (1 + r)n NPV = PV of inflows − cost. Positive NPV → accept. IRR = the discount rate where NPV = 0.

For a bond, IRR is yield to maturity. If a bond trades at a discount, IRR exceeds the coupon; at a premium, IRR is below it.

Risk and return

Beta — volatility vs. the market. Market = 1.0. Beta 1.2 → 20% more volatile. Alpha = actual return − expected return expected = rf + β(market return − rf) Sharpe = (portfolio return − risk-free rate) ÷ standard deviation CAPM = rf + β(market return − rf)

Sharpe uses standard deviation (total risk) in the denominator; Treynor uses beta (systematic risk). Picking the wrong denominator is the usual miss.

Correlation and diversification

Correlation runs −1.0 to +1.0. Diversification benefit rises as correlation falls; at exactly +1.0 there is no benefit at all. Standard deviation measures total risk; beta measures systematic (non-diversifiable) risk. Diversification reduces unsystematic risk only.

One standard deviation covers ~68% of outcomes, two ~95%, three ~99.7%.

Financial ratios

Current ratio = current assets ÷ current liabilities Quick ratio = (current assets − inventory) ÷ current liabilities Debt-to-equity = total debt ÷ total equity P/E = market price ÷ earnings per share P/B = market price ÷ book value per share Working capital = current assets − current liabilities

The quick ratio is the current ratio with inventory stripped out — the “acid test”. A high P/E signals a growth expectation; a low P/B is the value screen.

Returns

Current yield = annual income ÷ current market price Total return = (income + capital gain) ÷ beginning value Holding period return= (ending value − beginning + income) ÷ beginning Real (inflation-adj.)= nominal return − inflation rate After-tax return = nominal × (1 − tax rate) Risk-free real rate ≈ T-bill rate − CPI

Time-weighted return measures the manager — it strips out the effect of client deposits and withdrawals. Dollar-weighted (which is an IRR) measures the client's actual experience because it includes cash flow timing. Asked which judges a portfolio manager's skill, the answer is time-weighted.

Rule of 72

years to double ≈ 72 ÷ rate of return

Topics II & III — the lines that get tested

Heavy overlap with the Series 7, so this is deliberately short. It covers the places where the Series 66 asks differently.

Duration

Longer maturity and lower coupon both mean longer duration and more interest-rate sensitivity. A zero-coupon bond's duration equals its maturity. Given a choice, the most volatile bond is the longest-maturity, lowest-coupon one.

Retirement plans — post-SECURE 2.0

NASAA updated retirement questions effective 12 June 2023 for SECURE 2.0. Confirm your Kaplan material is post-2023 — if a practice question uses an RMD age of 70½ or 72, it is stale.

  • RMD age 73 (rising to 75 in 2033). No RMDs from a Roth IRA during the owner's life; SECURE 2.0 removed RMDs from Roth 401(k)s as well
  • Inherited IRAs for most non-spouse beneficiaries: the 10-year rule
  • Catch-up contributions indexed; higher catch-up for ages 60–63
  • 529-to-Roth rollover permitted, lifetime cap $35,000, 15-year account seasoning

Traditional IRA: deductible depending on income and plan coverage, taxed at withdrawal, 10% penalty before 59½. Roth: no deduction, qualified withdrawals tax-free after 5 years and 59½; contributions (not earnings) come out first and are always tax-free.

Trading terms

A buy stop sits above the market, a sell stop below. Stop orders become market orders when triggered — which is why a stop-limit can go unfilled. Payment for order flow must be disclosed. Best execution is an obligation independent of commission level.
